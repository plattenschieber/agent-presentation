### Description

An agent can produce a convincing result long before it has produced enough evidence to ship.

Agents Anonymous Cologne — Round 7 is about the gate between **“the agent says it is done”** and **“we are willing to merge, deploy, or delegate onward.”** We will compare the proof experienced practitioners actually use: acceptance criteria, executable checks, traces, screenshots, independent review, failure injection, risk ownership, and recovery plans.

This round is deliberately advanced. It is designed for people who have built, shipped, or operated a real agent system or workflow and can discuss the decisions around its output. You do not need a particular job title, framework, model, or vendor. We select for specific hands-on experience and a willingness to share what worked, what failed, and what changed your mind.

**What makes the room different:**

- Tool- and vendor-agnostic discussion
- A private, unrecorded atmosphere: no recording or live stream
- Five-minute participant lightning talks
- Concrete receipts over setup tours: tests, traces, reviews, screenshots, incidents, and “green but wrong” stories
- Open discussion about where human judgment still belongs

Bring one piece of evidence you trust, one signal you distrust, or one failure that forced you to redefine “done.”

**Lightning talks are welcome.** Keep it to five minutes and one or two slides. Show one receipt, one claim, or one useful failure. Speakers get a Kefir. It is tradition.

**Who should apply:**

Practitioners with hands-on experience building, shipping, or operating agent systems and workflows. This is an advanced session, but not a framework club: concrete experience matters more than tool choice, company, or title.

### Registration questions

Mark questions 1 and 2 as required. The wording is intentionally stack-neutral.

1. **Tell us about one agent system or workflow you have built, shipped, or operated.** What did it do, who used it, and what was your responsibility? Please be concrete; two to five sentences are enough.
2. **How did you decide its work was good enough?** Name the evidence you used—for example acceptance criteria, tests, traces, screenshots, review, production signals, or a human decision. If a “green” result still failed, we would value that story too.
3. **Optional: Share a profile or project link** that helps us understand your work. GitHub, LinkedIn, a personal site, or another relevant link is fine.
4. **Optional: Would you like to give a five-minute lightning talk?** Share a working title, the claim or failure you want to show, and the receipt you can bring. Keep it tool-agnostic, one or two slides, and free of vendor pitches. Kefir guaranteed.

### Admission note for reviewers

Prioritize specific evidence of hands-on building or operation and an answer that can enrich the room. Do not rank applicants by framework, model, employer, follower count, or polished writing. An honest failure with a concrete lesson is as relevant as a successful system.
